You need a website and you're torn: go with an agency or hire a freelance developer? Both can deliver a good site. The real question is which gives you the best result for your budget and your reality as a small business. Here are the concrete differences.

## Price

An agency carries heavy fixed costs: offices, account managers, sales teams. Those costs end up on your invoice. For the same brochure site, you often pay a premium just for the agency's structure.

A freelancer doesn't carry that overhead. You pay for the work, not the org chart. For most small businesses, that means an equivalent-quality site for a fraction of the price. If you want benchmarks, read my [website pricing guide](/en/blog/website-cost-quebec).

## Who you actually talk to

At an agency, your contact is often an account manager. Your message passes through them before it reaches the person who codes. Every round trip adds delay and room for misunderstanding.

With a freelancer, you talk directly to the person designing and building your site. No middleman, no broken telephone. Decisions happen fast and your vision reaches the final product intact.

## Speed of delivery

Agencies juggle several large accounts and heavy internal processes. A small project can sit behind bigger mandates.

A freelancer takes on fewer projects at once and can often start sooner. Most brochure sites I deliver go live in 2 to 4 weeks.

## When an agency makes sense

Let's be honest: an agency is the right call in some cases. If you run a large brand with dozens of pages, many stakeholders, ongoing ad campaigns and a need for multi-team support, an agency's capacity is justified.

For a small business, a local shop or a professional who wants a fast, well-ranked site that generates customers, a specialized freelancer is almost always the better value.

## What actually matters, either way

The label on the door matters less than these three things: the site's speed, its SEO structure and the person doing the work. A slow site that's invisible on Google earns you nothing, whether it came from an agency or a freelancer.
