Speed isn't a technical detail reserved for developers. It's a business issue. Every extra second of load time means visitors closing the tab before they ever see what you offer.

## The real cost of a slow site

The research has been consistent for years: the slower a site, the more people leave. Past about 3 seconds of load time, a large share of your visitors bail out. On mobile, where connections are often weaker, it's even worse.

For a small business, that means lost quote requests, abandoned carts, and calls that never happen. You paid to bring those people to your site, and the slowness sends them straight to your competitor.

## Google rewards fast sites

Speed doesn't only affect your visitors, it affects your position in Google too. **Core Web Vitals**, the performance metrics Google tracks, are part of the ranking signals. A fast, stable, responsive site has a real edge over a slow one in search results.

In other words: a slow site costs you twice. It drives away the people who arrive, and it keeps you from being found by everyone else.

## Why so many sites are slow

Most slow sites are slow for the same reasons:

- A generic theme bloated with features you don't use.
- A pile of plugins stacking up and slowing each other down.
- Huge images shipped with no optimization.
- Code that blocks the content from showing while the browser waits.

None of these are inevitable. They're technical choices, and they can be made differently.

## How you get a genuinely fast site

Here's the approach I apply on every project:

1. **Static HTML on first paint.** The important content shows up immediately, without waiting for JavaScript.
2. **Optimized images.** Modern formats (WebP, AVIF), explicit dimensions, lazy loading for anything lower on the page.
3. **Minimal JavaScript.** Animations and effects load only when needed, never at the expense of content.
4. **Preloaded fonts.** No text flashing or jumping as the page loads.

The result is a site that targets a perfect Lighthouse score and Core Web Vitals all in the green. Not for show: so your customers stay and Google takes notice.

## Bottom line

A beautiful site that loads slowly is a poor investment. Speed should be a baseline, not an option. If your current site drags, that's exactly the kind of problem that gets fixed, and that changes your results.
